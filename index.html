StitchMeter

A fully mechanical, flexure-based surgical training device for measuring suture-knot tightening force and knot-pull resistance — designed to avoid friction in the measurement chain and work without electronics.

Project at a glance

From surgical force to readable mechanical displacement

The design translates small suture forces into repeatable mechanical readings while minimizing friction and avoiding electronic sensing.

Design challenge

Measure two different suture forces with one robust device

StitchMeter measures the force applied while tightening a knot and the force required to pull the knot apart during a traction test.

  • Keep the force-measurement path free of conventional bearings and sliders.
  • Support three standard thread types with different stiffness ranges.
  • Remain low-tech, readable, and suitable for repeated use.

Core principles

A flexure-based mechanical architecture

The two measurement phases are mechanically separated so each can be dimensioned around its own force range and sensing principle.

01

Phase I — Knot tightening

The tightening force flexes parallel guiding blades. Lever amplification converts the displacement into a readable scale value, with force recovered using Hooke's law.

02

Phase II — Traction test

A separate mobile block tensions the knot through a stacked parallel-blade spring table. Relative displacement helps compensate for knot relaxation, while a trailing needle records the peak force.

03

Adjustable stiffness

Phase I uses an asymmetric rotating cam to buckle 0, 1, or 2 preload blades. Phase II varies the preload of a vertical spring to change the effective horizontal stiffness.

Construction choices

Materials were selected around stiffness, fatigue, manufacturability, wear, and visibility of the mechanism.

Aluminum 7075Main monolithic housing and flexure blades.
Böhler K190 tool steelCam, shaft, and spring components at wear interfaces.
Titanium 6Al-4VPhase II measurement-needle pivot.
PolycarbonateTransparent enclosure panels that also act as mechanical end-stops.

Key results

What the final design achieved

01

Flexure-only measurement chain

The force path relies on flexible elements, avoiding friction between the knot force and the needle reading.

02

Three thread types

A single device covers EP2, EP6, and EP9 using discrete buckling in Phase I and an adjustable spring in Phase II.

03

Resolution within specification

The final design stays within the specified force-resolution limit across the operating range for all three thread types.

04

Robust, low-tech architecture

The design is fully mechanical, does not require calibration after correct assembly, and is dimensioned for repeated use.
